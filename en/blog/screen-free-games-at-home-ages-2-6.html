Screen-free games to share at home

Pick an idea, gather simple objects and adapt the game to what feels fun today.

By Mundo Tuni. Need something to play at home without reaching for a phone? Pick one of these six games, gather the materials and let your child change the plan. These are Mundo Tuni play ideas to share, with no time target or skill to achieve.

Before you start

The NHS suggests using things you already have at home and joining in. It also says to follow toys’ age labels and not give a toy marked unsuitable for under-3s to a child under 3. That general guidance does not validate these six games or their outcomes.

Choose materials suitable for your child and stay with them. None of these games needs beads, coins, batteries, balloons or small parts. Remove anything that goes into your child’s mouth. Clear the space and avoid fragile items or sharp edges.

1. Find the sock’s partner

Gather: two pairs of large, clean socks that look different from each other.

  1. Put one sock from each pair in front of you and keep the others beside you.
  2. Offer a sock and ask, “Shall we put it with this one or this one?”
  3. Bring the matching sock closer to compare, then let your child pair them or invent a different arrangement.

Change it: start by showing a single pair. Add a third pair if your child asks for more. You can sit together and point instead of moving the socks.

2. A stuffed-animal shop

Gather: two or three stuffed animals suitable for your child’s age and an open box without staples.

  1. Set out the animals as if they were in a shop.
  2. Ask for one: “I’d like the bear, please.”
  3. Swap roles so your child orders and you serve, or let an animal be the customer.

Change it: name the shop or give one animal to each customer. There is no need for coins or correct quantities. For counting ideas, see our guide to counting through play.

3. A story with three things

Gather: a stuffed animal, a sock and an open box.

  1. Start: “A bear found a sock inside a box.”
  2. Ask “What happens next?” or wait to see what your child does with the objects.
  3. Follow their idea: the sock could be a bed, a scarf or something else entirely.

Change it: tell the story with gestures or sounds. Your child does not need to answer or finish a story. Our ideas for talking during play offer other ways to join in.

4. A tabletop echo

Gather: a stable table and your hands.

  1. Tap gently twice with your palm, then pause.
  2. Invite your child to respond however they like: a tap, a clap or a gesture.
  3. Let them invent the next sound and take your turn responding.

Change it: play silently by raising your hands. Repeat a sequence if that is fun, or make a new one each time. Copying without mistakes is not the goal.

5. A color museum

Gather: three large, safe toys or items of clothing with visible colors.

  1. Put out one object and say, “Today we’re opening a museum with this.”
  2. Offer two more objects and let your child choose which to display beside it.
  3. Tour the museum and point to what you like: give the exhibits names, sounds or a story.

Change it: find another item of the same color or group things using a rule your child invents. Bring objects over one at a time if moving around is not comfortable. For an optional Spanish twist, explore our Spanish color words; no new language is needed to play.

6. Paths for a stuffed animal

Gather: two small towels and an age-appropriate stuffed animal, on a clear floor.

  1. Lay the towels apart as two islands.
  2. Move the animal from one to the other and describe its visit.
  3. Let your child choose the next route or guide it with their hand.

Change it: use an accessible table. The character moves; nobody needs to jump onto the towels. Add a third island or pack up after the first visit.

Choosing and finishing

Offer two options: “The shop or the museum?” If neither appeals, leave them for another day. To finish, return each object to its place; the animal can wave goodbye if your child wants. There is no need to complete all six games or turn them into a daily routine.

This guide is for home. See our car and waiting-room games for travel ideas and their safety context. The suggestions here have not been evaluated as an educational or health intervention.
