Our commitments

What we promise, and how we checked it

Every sentence on this page has been checked against the app’s code or our privacy policy. Where there’s a catch, we say so.

Checked on November 10, 2026, against versions 2.4.2 and 2.4.3 of the app for iPhone and iPad.

What you won’t find in Mundo Tuni

  • No ads

    There are no ads of any kind inside the app.

  • No sign-up or accounts

    There’s no account to create. The app doesn’t ask for anyone’s name, email or date of birth. It only asks, and you can skip it, for your child’s approximate age, and saves an age group (2, 3, or 4 to 6) on the device.

  • No cross-app tracking

    The app doesn’t use the iPhone advertising identifier (IDFA) or Android’s, and it never asks for permission to track you across apps.

    Worth knowing: it does use Apple Ads’ standard measurement, which doesn’t need that permission. We explain it below, under “What leaves the device”.

  • Nothing watching how they play

    There are no behavior analytics tools such as Google Analytics or Firebase. The few counters the app keeps, such as how many times the purchase screen has opened, stay on the device.

  • No streaks, countdowns or daily goals

    Nobody loses anything by skipping a day, and Tuni never gets sad or pushes them to come back. Stars, stickers and aquarium friends are earned by playing and never expire.

    Worth knowing: there’s an optional daily reminder for grown-ups. It’s off by default and can only be turned on in the grown-up area.

  • No chat, no content from the internet

    There’s no chat, no share buttons and no social sign-in. Everything your child sees and hears is built into the app.

How they play

  • Mistakes aren’t punished

    When they get something wrong, Tuni cheers them on (“So close… Try again!”) and, from the second mistake, gives a hint. No stars are lost and there’s no error sound.

  • Your child chooses

    Your child decides which world and which game to open.

    Worth knowing: some days Tuni suggests reviewing something they played recently and opens it himself, and in the first few days he may take them to an island that suits their age. They can leave with the back arrow and pick something else.

  • No autoplay

    Each number, letter or color has a short series of games that ends with a celebration. To move on, they have to tap: nothing chains on endlessly.

    Worth knowing: if you don’t set a play time, the app doesn’t end the session on its own.

  • Works offline

    All the games work without internet, including in airplane mode.

    Worth knowing: buying and restoring a purchase need a connection. When there is one, the app checks the purchase as it opens (see below).

  • Made for small hands, and more

    The cells for choosing within each world are at least 72 points (96 on the iPad) and the back button is at least 48. There are subtitles with what Tuni says and symbols in the Colors world for kids who have trouble telling colors apart, which you turn on in the grown-up area; and it follows the system’s “Reduce Motion” setting.

    Worth knowing: the rest of the buttons and the objects you tap inside the games adapt to the screen size, and we haven’t measured a common minimum for them; we haven’t tested it with VoiceOver or TalkBack yet, and system text only grows up to 130%.

What you decide

  • How long they play

    There’s no limit by default. When you start, and any time in the grown-up area, you can choose 5, 10 or 15 minutes of play. When time is up, Tuni finishes the game in progress, says goodbye, suggests an idea to keep playing without a screen and goes to sleep. For the next hour, a grown-up is needed to play again.

    Worth knowing: it’s a help to end calmly, not a recommendation of how much screen time to allow.

  • Purchases, settings and links behind a grown-up gate

    To get in, you solve an addition or a multiplication meant for grown-ups. If your child taps something locked, Tuni says it’s closed and takes them to a free game: he never asks them to buy anything or to go and get someone. If they tap it again, the gate appears.

    Worth knowing: the gate makes it hard for a young child, but it isn’t a password. Payment is always confirmed by the store (Apple or Google).

  • The microphone, only if you turn it on

    It comes switched off. If you turn it on in the grown-up area, in Lola’s corner your child hears their own voice when repeating a word. The recording is kept briefly on the device, played once and deleted. It isn’t transcribed or sent anywhere.

  • What each world practices

    In the grown-up area, each world explains what it practices, and you see what your child has done, concept by concept, with no rankings or comparisons.

    Worth knowing: these are the goals each world was designed around, not measured results.

  • Ideas for play without a screen

    If you set a play time, Tuni ends with an idea to keep going at home, like counting plates while setting the table or finding three things of one color. Lola’s corner also has “Ideas for home” in the grown-up area.

What leaves the device, and to whom

We won’t tell you that no data leaves the device, because that wouldn’t be true. Here’s what happens.

Stays on your device

  • Progress, stars, stickers and aquarium friends.
  • The age group and every setting.
  • The usage counters in the grown-up area.
  • Your child’s voice, if you use the microphone, and only until it plays.

Leaves the device

RevenueCat (USA), which handles purchases on our behalf
Every time the app opens with a connection: a random identifier created by its own system, with no name or email, and technical connection data such as the IP address and the app and system versions. If you buy or restore, also the details of that purchase provided by the store.
Apple, to measure our ads (iPhone and iPad only)
When the app opens, RevenueCat receives an attribution code from Apple and asks Apple whether the install came from one of our Apple Ads ads. It keeps the campaign details (campaign, ad group and keyword), which we use only to see, in aggregate, which ads work. It doesn’t use the IDFA and can’t be used to follow anyone across apps. It happens before the grown-up gate, without your child seeing or choosing anything.
Apple and Google, as app stores
They see downloads, purchases and ratings under their own policies, and show us aggregate statistics.

And only if you decide to: whatever you write to hola@mundotuni.com, and the purchase-profile deletion request the grown-up area prepares, which you send yourself.

This website doesn’t use cookies. We count visits with Cloudflare Web Analytics, which, according to Cloudflare, doesn’t use cookies either, and we only see aggregate figures.

Artificial intelligence: where we use it and where we don’t

We use artificial intelligence tools to create part of what’s in the app. The “Credits” screen in the grown-up area has more detail.

  • The voices of Tuni, in all his languages, and Lola’s: generated with ElevenLabs.
  • Sound effects and music: generated with ElevenLabs.
  • Tuni’s two animations (the spin and the wave): generated with Kling, inside ElevenLabs, from Tuni’s drawing.
  • The illustrations, such as the logo, icons, backgrounds, stickers, aquarium creatures and word pictures: created with ChatGPT.
  • Some videos on our social media for parents include shots and voices generated with AI.

All of this was created while we built the app and ships inside it. While your child plays, the app doesn’t use any artificial intelligence service and sends nothing to ElevenLabs, OpenAI or any other AI service.

Nor does it listen to understand what your child says: if you turn on the microphone, the app only measures the volume to know when they start and stop talking.

What we don’t promise

  • We don’t promise your child will learn more or sooner. We haven’t run studies with children, and Mundo Tuni isn’t a course or a therapy.
  • It doesn’t replace playing, reading or talking with you. The most it can do is not get in the way.
  • We don’t claim it has been reviewed by pediatricians or educators, and it has no seals or certificates from anyone.
  • We don’t say that no data leaves the device: above you’ll find what does and where it goes.
  • We don’t promise it’s perfect. If something breaks or doesn’t match this page, we want to know.

What the WHO and pediatricians say about screens

The World Health Organization recommends that, for ages 2 to 4, sedentary screen time “should be no more than 1 hour; less is better.” Before age 2, it doesn’t recommend it.

The American Academy of Pediatrics doesn’t set a number of minutes; it asks those of us who make apps to turn off autoplay and avoid designs that prolong engagement. Spain’s pediatric association goes further and advises avoiding screens from 0 to 6, because there is no safe amount of time.

That’s why Mundo Tuni won’t tell you how much time is good: that’s your call.

Sources: WHO, April 24, 2019 · American Academy of Pediatrics, January 20, 2026 · Spanish Association of Pediatrics, Plan Digital Familiar (in Spanish)
